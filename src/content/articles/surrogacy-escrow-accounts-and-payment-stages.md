Most people read a surrogacy quote as a price. The document that decides what happens to your money is not the quote. It is the agreement, and inside it, the escrow terms.

The reason this deserves separate treatment is timing. Money in a surrogacy program moves early and in one direction, and the point at which it becomes difficult to get back is usually well before anything medical has happened.

## The structure, in four parts

[Professional guidance from the bar association of adoption and assisted reproduction attorneys](https://adoptionart.org/assisted-reproduction/financial-terms-escrow-accounts/) sets out the standard arrangement, and it is worth reading as four separate propositions:

1. **Every arrangement should be formalised by a written agreement.** Not an invoice, not a package description plus an email thread.
2. **The agreement should contain the complete financial agreement** between the intended parents and the surrogate. Complete means the amounts, the schedule, and what happens when the plan changes.
3. **The escrow account is typically funded before the first embryo transfer.** Your money is committed at the start of treatment, not at the birth.
4. **The written agreement tells the parties how much money should be in escrow.** This is the number that determines your exposure while the program is live.

The same guidance states that the escrow should be **neutral**. That word is doing real work: it means an account that disburses on agreed terms rather than a balance held by whichever party has the most to gain from releasing it slowly or keeping it.

## Why the carrier's own lawyer changes the picture

Independent representation is not a courtesy. [Academic review of US practice records that separate legal representation for each party is recommended and sometimes mandated](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11621256/), and that in some jurisdictions carrier contracts are unenforceable or void.

Read those two facts together and the structure of the risk becomes clear. If the contract is unenforceable where you signed it, the escrow terms and the parties' good faith are what actually protect the money. And if the other side had no independent advice when they agreed to those terms, the arrangement is weaker at exactly the moment you would need it to hold.

This is also why "the agency handled the paperwork" is not a sufficient answer to a financial question. One party cannot represent both sides' interests and cannot hold the money and decide its release.

## The five questions that expose your actual exposure

Ask each of these of the agency, the escrow provider and your lawyer separately, and compare the three answers. Where they differ, you have found the clause to renegotiate.

1. **What amount must be in escrow, and by what date?** Not "when it is needed". A date, tied to a medical event.
2. **Who authorises each disbursement, and what happens if you disagree with one?** If a single party can release or withhold, the account is not neutral in practice, whatever it is called.
3. **What happens to money still in escrow if the pregnancy ends?** Distinguish early loss, later loss, and a live birth that goes differently than planned. These are three different clauses.
4. **What is refunded, and from where?** An agency fee refunded from the agency's own balance depends on the agency. Money still sitting in escrow does not.
5. **Who pays escrow administration, and does the fee continue if the program pauses?** A monthly charge on a balance you have already committed is a cost you keep paying during a stall.

## Payment stages, mapped to medical events

A schedule worth reading is one where each payment is tied to an event you can verify, rather than to a date. Build a two-column list: the medical milestone, and what becomes payable and non-refundable at that point.

Typical milestones to check against: signing the agreement, funding escrow, starting medication, the transfer itself, a confirmed pregnancy, viability, delivery, and the parentage order. Ask specifically what is due between a confirmed pregnancy and the birth, because that is the window where most of the carrier-side reimbursement is spent and where a program can still stop.

## What this does not solve

Escrow protects the movement of money. It does not decide enforceability, parentage, or whether the arrangement holds if someone withdraws. And nothing about a well-run escrow tells you whether the program behind it is well run; that is the [document review in the agency guide](/guides/choosing-a-surrogacy-agency).

If you have two proposals in front of you, the [comparison tool](/tools/agency-quote-comparison) sets both against the same nine costs, with amounts left blank wherever a quote does not state one. The number you are comparing has to be the number you can name a payee for.

What is described above is how these arrangements are generally structured, based on professional guidance and its date. It is not legal or financial advice, and the terms that govern you are in your own agreement.
