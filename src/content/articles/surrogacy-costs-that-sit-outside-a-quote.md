A package price is a description of what a provider sells. It is not a description of what you will pay. The difference between those two numbers is usually the difference between a budget that held and one that did not.

This is the list to work through with a quote in front of you. For each item, ask the provider to state in writing whether it is included, excluded, or billed by someone else. "Included" means nothing unless you can name the payee.

## Medical items that follow the treatment, not the package

1. **Additional transfer attempts.** A package commonly covers one transfer. Ask what the second costs, what a frozen-thaw cycle costs, and whether medication is priced separately each time.
2. **Donor eggs or sperm.** The largest single scope change, and frequently outside the quoted program because it requires a separate cycle, a separate laboratory and a separate donor arrangement.
3. **Pharmacy and monitoring.** Bloodwork, ultrasounds and medication are often billed by the clinic as they occur rather than quoted in advance.
4. **Screening that has to be repeated.** If a candidate carrier fails screening after you paid for matching, ask whether the fee covers a new candidate and how many times.
5. **The carrier's prenatal care and delivery.** Who bills for them, and whose insurance answers first, is a question with a specific answer that changes the total substantially.
6. **Neonatal care.** A hospital bill for a premature infant is not a surrogacy cost by any definition, which is exactly why it never appears in a surrogacy quote.

## Legal and administrative items

7. **Independent counsel for the carrier.** Separate representation for each party is [recommended and sometimes mandated](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11621256/) in US practice. It is a real line item and it is not your lawyer's fee.
8. **Court and filing costs for the parentage route**, including what happens if a judge requires further hearings or a post-birth proceeding instead of a pre-birth order.
9. **Escrow administration.** A monthly or per-disbursement fee, often quoted as a minimum balance requirement rather than a cost. The professional standard is that the [escrow is funded before the first embryo transfer](https://adoptionart.org/assisted-reproduction/financial-terms-escrow-accounts/), so this cost begins before any child exists.
10. **Registration, translation and apostille.** Getting the child's documents issued and accepted usually involves third parties outside every program description.

## Personal items that programs describe as "out of scope"

11. **Your travel and stay**, including how many trips, how long you must be present, and who else comes. Transfers and birth dates are set by clinic schedules, not by your leave calendar.
12. **The carrier's reimbursable expenses**, wherever you are. In Canada the regulator enumerates them, and [the list](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/programs/consultation-reimbursement-assisted-human-reproduction/document.html) runs from travel, childcare and counselling through medications, delivery, insurance and the income lost around the pregnancy, with receipts required and records kept for six years. In the United Kingdom what may lawfully be paid is the carrier's [expenses](https://www.hfea.gov.uk/about-us/media-centre/surrogacy-a-factsheet/), and a judge has to authorise anything beyond them before a court will hand over the child's parentage. Either way, the category is real, and in a quote it is frequently summarised as one line or omitted entirely.

## What one provider's split says about the width of a band

[The cost guide](/guides/surrogacy-cost) quotes one clinic's own division of a total into an agency fee, a base compensation range and a legal cost range ([Illume Fertility](https://www.illumefertility.com/fertility-blog/average-cost-of-surrogacy-in-the-united-states)). Those are the provider's numbers with no disclosed methodology, and they are worth reading here for their width rather than their level: the top of one band and the bottom of the next together exceed most of the items on the list above.

## How to work through this without losing the thread

Put the quote into the [comparison tool](/tools/agency-quote-comparison) as one row per item on this list. Set the scope to included, excluded, or needs checking. Leave the amount blank when the quote does not state it, and keep it marked as needs checking: unconfirmed amounts stay out of the total, which is the point. A total that quietly assumes nothing is missing is the least useful number you can produce.

Then take the rows still marked needs checking into the [agency checklist](/guides/choosing-a-surrogacy-agency) and turn each into a written question for a named party: agency, lawyer, clinic or escrow provider.

That is the whole method. The gap between a quoted number and a real budget is not closed by finding a better average. It is closed by listing what the quote did not mention.

This page is general information about cost structure. It is not a quote, an estimate for your situation, or legal or financial advice.
